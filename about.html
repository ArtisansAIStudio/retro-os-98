About Basement 98

Remember the last bench, the back page of your rough copy, a phone with one game and forty minutes of battery? Basement 98 is a small love letter to all of that, running in a web page.

Open Basement 98

What it is

Basement 98 looks and behaves like a computer desktop from the late 1990s. You get a boot screen, a Start menu, windows you can drag and resize, a taskbar with a clock, and icons on the desktop. Inside it there are about two dozen games, a handful of small programs, and a few things we are not going to tell you about.

There is nothing to install and no account to make. Open the page, wait for the boot screen, and start playing.

The name is a joke. There are no windows down here.

What is inside

  • Notebook classics we all played at school: FLAMES, Book Cricket, Name Place Animal Thing, Hangman, Stone Paper Scissors, Dots and Boxes and Tic-Tac-Toe.
  • Desktop classics: Minesweeper and Solitaire.
  • Arcade-style games written from scratch: a split-screen racer, a first-person maze shooter, a block-building sandbox, falling blocks, Pong, a run-and-gun, a brick breaker, an alien shooter, a maze chase, a road-crossing game, an asteroid blaster and a motorbike race-and-fight game.
  • Small programs: Notepad (your files are saved on your device), Paint, a calculator, a DOS-style prompt, a media player that can play your own music files, and a web browser that dials up the 1998 internet.
  • Sweets. Win games and you collect sweets in My Sweets. There are 14 to find, and one of them is hidden.
  • A worldwide Hall of Fame with leaderboards, a daily challenge that is the same for everyone on Earth, and a live feed of scores.

See the game guides for controls and tips for every game.

Everything is original

All the code, artwork and sounds were written for this project. The games are our own versions of classic ideas, such as a car race, a maze shooter and a block puzzle. They are inspired by the era but are not copies of any commercial game, and the site does not host anyone else's games. The optional Game Cabinet only plays game files that visitors load themselves from their own device.

Fair play

Scores on the leaderboard are checked by a server. When a game starts the server issues a one-time token, and for some games it replays the moves you made to confirm the score is real. It is not perfect, but it keeps the boards honest.

Who makes it

Basement 98 is a hobby project from the owner of artisansmedia.com. It is free to play and may show ads to cover its costs, only if you allow them. Read the privacy policy for exactly what is stored and why.
